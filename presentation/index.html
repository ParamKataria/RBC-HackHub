Intro · RBC Hackathon

Six doors into Black history.

Black Cultural Centre for Nova Scotia. Cherry Brook. Established 1983.

Problem Statement 1 · 10 minutes01

Problem

Black history in Canada is held in many places. A visitor should not have to know which one to open first.

Museums, archives, libraries, universities, governments, and community collections each keep their own catalogues, words, and access rules. Useful material sits in finding aids, PDFs, websites, and local databases. Much of it is only partly digitized.

What BCCNS asked for

A trusted discovery layer. It should point to the institution that holds the record, keep that institution’s ownership, and make the fragments easier to find and connect. A small organization has to be able to keep it going.

There is no single catalogue today02

Problem

A catalogue asks you to arrive with a query. Most people arrive wanting to learn.

Someone interested in African Nova Scotian history may not know to start at Nova Scotia Archives, the Black Loyalist Heritage Centre, Library and Archives Canada, or the Centre’s own site. A results list helps once the name, the place, or the year is already in mind.

The first screen had to teach

We wanted an informational visit: chapters you can walk, records you can read, and a question you can ask about the place you have stopped. The catalogue stays available when a query is the right tool.

An experience for anyone who wants to learn03

Solution · what it is allowed to say

A question is answered only when the sources answer it.

“No records in the archive match this question.”

01Search

The words become filters against the catalog: institution, type, subject, place, keywords.

02Where you are

The gallery or the record in view is included, because that is what you are looking at.

03Sentences

Each sentence must cite record ids from that set. At most four. A sentence with an unknown id is dropped.

04Or stop

If the set does not contain the fact, the answer says so. It does not fill the gap from general knowledge.

How it works · the shared record

Normalize the label. Keep the owner on the row.

Each source has a mapping sheet onto Dublin Core: title, creator, date, type, place, rights, access, provenance, and the institution’s own URL. The original row is stored unchanged. People, places, and subjects are linked names. Full-text search covers title, description, subject, and place.

What stays empty

No source in the sample has a community field, so that column stays empty. Birchtown remains a place or a subject. A date that is not a calendar day stays as text. The hub links to the holding institution. It does not copy the collection.
